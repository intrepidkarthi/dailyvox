# What Is a Shared Journal? Best Apps and How They Work

A shared journal is a single diary where two or more people contribute entries to the same feed. Couples use them for relationship check-ins. Parents use them to record milestones. Friends use them to stay connected across time zones.

If you came here looking to build a shared journal inside DailyVox, stop reading and download Day One instead. DailyVox does not support shared journals. It has no user accounts, no central servers, and no pipeline to send an entry from your phone to someone else. 

Shared journaling requires networked software. Here is how it works, which apps actually do it, and why writing for another person changes what you put on the page.

## The Best Apps for Shared Journaling

Dedicated shared journaling apps are rare because syncing multi-user text with media is technically demanding. Most people settle for general collaboration tools, but a few dedicated options exist.

### 1. Day One
Day One has a feature built specifically for this: Shared Journals. You create a journal, invite another Day One user via a link, and both people can post text, photos, and voice notes into the same timeline. Entries display the contributor's name, and updates sync between accounts. It is the most polished dedicated shared journal available today. If you want a traditional journal feel with prompts, locations, and multimedia for two or more people, start here.

### 2. Apple Notes or Google Docs
They are unglamorous. They work. A shared folder in Apple Notes or a running document in Google Docs costs nothing and requires no new app install. You lose timeline views, voice transcription pipelines, and mood tracking. In return, you get real-time typing, comment threads, and zero platform lock-in. Many couples end up on Google Docs simply because one person uses Android and the other uses an iPhone.

### 3. Between
Between is a dedicated app for couples that includes a shared diary alongside calendars and private messaging. It is built around relationship history rather than deep reflection. If your goal is preserving memories, dates, and milestones together, it handles the job cleanly.

## Apps That Do Not Support Shared Journals

When searching for shared journals, search engines often surface single-user diary apps that lack collaborative features entirely. 

- **Apple Journal:** Apple Journal is strictly tied to a single iPhone and Apple ID. It has no sharing mechanics, no web interface, and no multi-user access. Apple built it as a personal, on-device diary.
- **Daylio:** Daylio is a micro-journaling and mood-tracking tool. You tap icons to log feelings and activities. It tracks individual patterns over time. You can export your data as a PDF, but you cannot invite a partner to contribute.
- **Rosebud:** Rosebud is an interactive journaling app focused on personalized AI guidance. Its system analyzes your personal entries to offer prompts and insights. It is structured entirely around individual self-discovery, not group conversation.

## The Problem with Writing for an Audience

A shared journal is not just a private journal with two authors. It is a completely different medium.

A private diary works because nobody is looking. You write ugly thoughts. You process petty irritations. You voice contradictions you would never say out loud. The page absorbs the mess without judgment, which helps you untangle what you actually feel.

The moment another person has access to the page, your brain shifts into performance mode. You self-censor. You soften complaints about your partner because you know they will read them before bed. You omit doubts because you do not want to cause worry. 

A shared journal is correspondence. It is closer to exchanging letters than keeping a diary. That has immense value for intimacy and record-keeping, but it rarely replaces the psychological utility of a private notebook.

## Why DailyVox Stays Strictly Solo

DailyVox is an open-source voice journaling app for iPhone and Android. It does not have shared journals, and it never will.

On Android, DailyVox requests zero internet permissions. It cannot talk to an outside server because the operating system physically blocks it from accessing the network. Speech recognition runs on the phone itself. 

On iPhone, DailyVox uses on-device speech processing frameworks. Data remains in the local app sandbox, with an option to sync to your private iCloud account. Android has no sync at all, and nothing syncs between an iPhone and an Android device. Moving a journal to a new phone requires generating an encrypted backup file and transferring it by hand. Android also lacks the AI chat, health data integration, and photo attachments available on iOS.

To make a shared journal work, we would need to collect user emails, build database servers, issue login tokens, and route private voice recordings through the cloud. Every server you add is a liability. Every account system is a breach waiting to happen. 

We chose total privacy over collaborative features. If you need a digital space to share thoughts with a partner, pick Day One. If you need a safe place to dump your raw voice notes where no other human can ever read them, use DailyVox.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can two people share a single journaling account?
Some people attempt to share a login on apps that sync across devices. This usually causes sync conflicts. If two people write at the same time, entries can overwrite each other or create duplicate database records. It is better to use an app with explicit multi-user permissions, like Day One, or a shared document.

### Does Apple Journal allow family sharing?
No. While Apple Journal is part of iOS, Apple does not allow family members to share a journal through Family Sharing. The entries are encrypted and tied strictly to your individual device and passcode.

### Can I share an entry from a private journal app?
Yes. Most private journal apps, including DailyVox, allow you to export individual entries. In DailyVox, you can export your text or copy transcripts directly to share a specific realization with a partner via text or email, without granting them ongoing access to your entire diary.
