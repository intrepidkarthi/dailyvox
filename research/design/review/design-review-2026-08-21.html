Fourteen findings, and only one of them is about colour.

Reviewed against Evergreen & Gold Hour and against six screenshots from a real device. The palette is sound. What keeps failing is the layer beneath it — where a decision gets made, whether a screen has one job or three, and whether two things on the same screen mean the same thing.

Failure class A · the decision made at the wrong layer

A colour picked at the call site instead of the token

Three of the six screenshots are one bug wearing different clothes. The palette is semantic and correct; the call sites reached past it for a literal day value, and a literal has no idea what ground it landed on.

A1Day tokens hardcoded onto surfaces that go navyFixed
On screen

“DailyVox is locked” was invisible — the only readable thing on the lock screen was an error. The Journal’s search, mic and filter icons all but disappeared. The “Daily reflection” prompt titles were unreadable.

Why

DS.Palette.ink measures 1.16:1 on navy and DS.Palette.sage measures 2.22:1. Both are correct values and neither is a decision — the decision belongs to theme.textColor, which knows the ground.

Call

Swept as a class rather than as three bugs: 17 call sites across seven files. The instances were never the problem — a lint rule banning DS.Palette.ink and .sage as foregrounds would close it permanently.

A2The day positive misses AA by 0.11Open
On screen

Not visible yet. #4A7C59 currently carries icons and large text only, where it is fine.

Why

4.39:1 on cream, against a 4.5:1 threshold. It was added to fix a worse bug — the night green was being used on both grounds — and landed just short.

Call

Darken to roughly #446F51. It clears AA and is visually indistinguishable at these sizes. Cheap now, awkward once it is load-bearing on body copy.

Failure class B · two things that mean one thing

The screen offers a choice it cannot resolve

The most damaging findings here, and none of them is a bug. Each is a place where the interface presents two of something and gives no way to tell which one is meant.

B1Two microphones on the Record screen, one of them inertBreaks
On screen

A green mic in an 80‑point circle sits inside the “Add a star to today’s sky” card. Forty points below it, the real gold record button. The card is not tappable.

Why

The card is an empty‑state illustration that chose a mic glyph. On the one screen whose entire job is to get someone talking in a few seconds, the first mic they see does nothing.

Call

Delete the card. B2 asks for a greeting, the question, the mic and — once an entry exists — the Today card. It has no slot for a second invitation to do the thing the button already invites.

B2Two searches, and the one worth having is hiddenBreaks
On screen

A system search bar labelled “Search entries”, revealed by pulling down. Separately, a magnifier icon in the header opening semantic search.

Why

Ordinary keyword search got the discoverable position and the plain name. Searching by meaning — the thing no competitor can copy, the thing the spec words as “Describe it — search what you meant” — is behind an unlabelled glyph.

Call

Promote semantic search to the visible field with the spec’s own copy, and drop .searchable. Two searches is one too many, and the app kept the wrong one.

B3“Your Twin” and “AI Insights” name the same thingBreaks
On screen

Entry detail carries “What your Twin filed ✦” and, directly beneath it, a card called “AI Insights”. Both are the machine’s reading of the same entry.

Why

The product has one name for this and it is your Twin — a thing that is yours and stays here. “AI” is the category the app defines itself against. Using both teaches people the Twin is a skin over something generic.

Call

One vocabulary. Fold the insights into the Twin ledger, or rename the card. A name that changes between two cards on one screen is not a name.

Failure class C · the shipped screen against the drawn one

Spec content arrived; legacy content never left

Each of these is additive drift. The specified elements were built correctly and placed underneath whatever was already there, so the screens read as accumulation rather than composition.

C1The Record screen’s top third belongs to neither the spec nor the taskDrift
On screen

Greeting, question, the “Add a star” card, an “A starting thought” prompt row, then a docked mic that leaves the prompts clipped mid‑card.

Why

Two unspecified sections take the space between the question and the answer. The scroll area is squeezed until its last element is always cut, which is why the prompts look broken rather than scrollable.

Call

Cut the card (see B1). Keep the prompts — they earn their place against the blank‑page problem — but move them above the question or into the recording state, where a starting thought is actually useful.

C2Four cards of identical weight, and the transcript is one of themDrift
On screen

Entry detail stacks a metadata card, the transcript, the Twin ledger and AI Insights — same radius, same fill, same elevation.

Why

The transcript is what the person came for; everything else is apparatus around it. Rendering all four identically means nothing is primary and the eye starts at the top, which is the date.

Call

Let the transcript sit on the page rather than in a card, at a larger size with real line height. The apparatus stays carded. One promotion, no new colours.

C3Three labels in the sky, two treatmentsDrift
On screen

MOTU sits in a filled gold chip. HEMA and TODAY are bare text at the same size.

Why

Named stars are one category. Two visual treatments imply a distinction that does not exist, and the reader spends attention looking for it.

Call

One treatment for all three. The chip reads better against a busy sky — use it, or drop it, but not both.

C4Four tabs, four unrelated header patternsDrift
On screen

Record: greeting, date, two stat chips, an unlabelled ellipsis. Journal: title plus three icons. Twin: title plus two icons. Entry detail: a circled chevron, a date, and a star sharing a pill with “Edit”.

Why

Each header was designed for its own screen. Moving between tabs, the top 80 points rearrange completely, so nothing above the content is learnable.

Call

One header rule — title left, actions right, same size and baseline — and let Record’s greeting be the single documented exception, since it is the only screen that greets. Split the star out of the Edit pill; they are unrelated actions in one container.

C5Two stacked chip rows before any contentDrift
On screen

Journal shows All / People / Mood / Body, then a second row of entity chips, then the first entry — roughly a third of the screen before a word of journal.

Why

Two filter systems at the same visual weight, one general and one specific, with nothing to say they are different kinds of thing.

Call

Keep the spec’s four. Move entity chips inside the People filter, where a reader has already said that is what they want.

Failure class D · closed this pass

Structure, and things that only a device could tell us

Listed for the record. All six were found on hardware, and none would have been caught by a build.

D1Tab targets were 31 points, against a 44‑point floorFixed
Why

A 10.5‑point label with 9 points of padding, and no contentShape, so the transparent part of an inactive tab was not hit‑testable at all. The bar also sat 4 points off the bottom, inside the strip where the system claims the first touch.

D2With a keyboard up, taps fell through the tab barFixed
Why

SwiftUI lifts the bar as a sibling of the focused view and its hit region does not follow. There was a bar on screen that could not be pressed. It now hides while typing, as it already did while recording.

D3The greeting scrolled into the clockFixed
Why

Every destination hides the navigation bar to draw its own header, leaving nothing between scrolling content and the status bar.

D4The lock screen accused people who had only pressed CancelFixed
Why

Authentication returned a boolean, which cannot tell a mismatch from a dismissal. The screen auto‑prompts on appear, so dismissing that prompt latched “Authentication failed” for the session.

D5The Twin screen showed two different countsFixed
Why

“137 ✦” above “from 45 entries”. Twin state and Core Data rows genuinely disagree — worth chasing, not worth showing both halves of.

D6Dimension stats clipped by the floating barFixed
Why

Clearance was computed from a declared bar height of 62 against a real height of about 45.
